Project Overview

What it is

Bon-Tress is a 2D pixel tower defense game where towers represent positive study habits and defend against Bons, enemies based on negative ones. It has 20+ levels plus a tutorial, each adding new towers and mechanics.

Why it was built

Built for Temasek Polytechnic Learning Academy with NUS and Singapore Polytechnic to teach self-directed learning (SDL) through gameplay. Tower defense was chosen for its natural fit with strategic thinking and problem-solving.

What it covered

Design was grounded in Bloom's Taxonomy and the 6 Learning Strategies. Alongside the main campaign, SDL minigames like Dual Coding and Retrieval Practice let players test and reinforce study techniques over time.

The project gave me hands-on experience designing serious games, where the goal goes beyond entertainment to measurable learning outcomes.

What I Did

Ideation and Prototyping

Led two prototype iterations, handling scoping decisions to fit the project timeline. Settling on a genre that suited the game's educational goals proved challenging early on, before pivoting to tower defense. Built the Adobe XD mockup and wrote the Game Design Documents to align the team early.

Game Design

Designed each Bon's traits and abilities. Balanced 20+ levels of difficulty by tracking enemy speed, damage, and wave composition. Researched and iterated on multiple scoring algorithms to quantify learning as productivity, measuring how effectively players applied specific strategies, resource setup, and timing to clear each wave interval.

Programming

Built Bon behaviour covering movement, ability triggers, and animation states. Used object pooling to optimize performance, and Scriptable Objects to let designers tune balancing values without touching code. Applied inheritance across Bons and towers for easy scalability as new types were added.

Playtesting and Handover

Ran playtests with local students via Google Forms. Used feedback on mid-game difficulty and combat clarity to add an in-game encyclopedia. Before graduation, cleaned up code comments and wrote documentation for a smooth handover.

What I Learned

Designing a serious game means staying aware of the learning goals first, then using gameplay to elevate the experience without losing the lesson. Balancing "fun" against "educational" proved genuinely hard. My approach was to isolate one strategy to teach at a time and refine it before layering on more.

In hindsight, the game itself should have included a built-in feature to track learning progress over time, validating whether goals were actually met and surfacing personalized support based on each player's outcomes.

Our first prototype wasn't playtested until quests, narrative, art, and audio were already built, too late to catch design flaws. Pivoting to Bon-Tress taught me to validate the core loop first and commit resources only once it's proven.

Letting players freely combine towers meant accounting for strategies we hadn't planned for. The scoring system took many iterations to stay fair across every possible approach.

Working with other programmers meant dealing with merge conflicts and overlapping changes. Using Scriptable Objects and base class inheritance made it much easier for teammates to extend features independently.
