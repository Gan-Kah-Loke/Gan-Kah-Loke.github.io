Bon-Tress is a 2D pixel tower defense game where players deploy towers embodying positive learning traits to fend off "Bons" — slime infiltrators representing negative study habits. Developed over nine months for Temasek Polytechnic Learning Academy in collaboration with NUS (research) and Singapore Polytechnic (art assets), the game was purpose-built to make Self-Directed Learning (SDL) engaging through strategic, systems-driven gameplay.

01

IDEATION & PROTOTYPING

Early Adobe XD prototype — validated before a single line of code was written

RESEARCH BASE

TWO FRAMEWORKS,
ONE GAME

Gamifying educational content demanded deep research before a single mechanic was designed. Every decision was grounded in Bloom's Taxonomy for measurable learning outcomes, and the 6 Learning Strategies by the Learning Scientists to embed the educational process into moment-to-moment play.

DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Tower defense naturally encourages critical thinking, problem-solving, and strategic planning — a direct mirror of self-directed learning. Players discover solutions rather than following predetermined paths.

02

DEVELOPMENT — TOWER DEFENSE

CORE LOOP

20 LEVELS OF
STRATEGIC DEPTH

The core loop spans 20 levels plus a tutorial, each progressively introducing new tower types and Bon variants. Players score higher by applying the embedded learning strategies effectively — skill expression is directly tied to educational objectives.

DESIGN

  • Designed Bon characteristics, traits, and abilities — each with a distinct gameplay role
  • Developed level progression and difficulty balancing with a smooth learning curve
  • Created a scoring system to quantify and measure learning with meaningful feedback

CODE

  • Implemented Bon behavior: movement, ability triggers, animation state machines
  • Built scoring logic with save/load functionality for persistent progress tracking
  • Designed Scriptable Objects & inheritable enemy base class for clean extensibility

In-game tower defense — towers embody positive learning traits, Bons represent negative habits

03

LEVEL DESIGN — THE MAPS

ENVIRONMENT 01

FOREST

The introductory environment. Winding paths teach players tower placement fundamentals while keeping enemy variety low to ease the learning curve.

ENVIRONMENT 02

DESERT

A more open layout with split-lane branching paths. Forces players to think economically — tower limits per level keep unanticipated strategies viable.

LEVEL DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Enemy stats were tracked in a spreadsheet against the in-game economy. Levels were deliberately open-ended: tower limits per stage let unanticipated strategies remain viable rather than forcing a single solution.

LEARNING

Six minigames were built alongside the tower defense core — each mapped 1:1 to one of the 6 Learning Strategies. They serve both as a complement to the main loop and a standalone practice tool for players to test and validate their own learning methods.

END NOTE

LOOKING BACK

Bon-Tress was an incredibly rewarding project, even with the challenge of restarting development mid-way through. It gave me a deeper appreciation for how much craft goes into making a game that is simultaneously engaging and educational — especially when tackling serious learning concepts that resist simplification.

Working with real institutional clients and cross-institution collaborators shaped how I think about design communication, rapid validation, and keeping systems open enough for teammates to build on freely. One day I hope to return to this project with refined skills to bring it to full completion.
